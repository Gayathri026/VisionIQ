# ASTM A568/A568M: Standard Specification for Steel Sheet Surface Quality

## Section 1: Surface Imperfections and Defect Taxonomy
Carbon and high-strength low-alloy hot-rolled and cold-rolled steel sheet is subject to manufacturing surface imperfections arising from continuous casting, reheating, hot rolling, pickling, or cold reduction:
- Crazing: Network of fine hairline fractures or micro-checking resulting from thermal cyclical fatigue or copper contamination during rolling.
- Rolled-In Scale: Oxide scale pressed mechanically into the steel sheet surface during continuous hot rolling, leaving embedded dark patches or pit lines.
- Inclusions: Non-metallic entrapped particles (silicates, aluminates) breaching the sheet surface.
- Scratches and Gouges: Longitudinal or abrasive mechanical marks caused by friction with guide rolls, de-coilers, or handling equipment.
- Pitted Surface: Micro-cavities formed by acid entrapment during pickling or localized galvanic corrosion.

## Section 2: Rejection Limits and Acceptability Criteria
Imperfections that do not impair structural integrity or fabrication capability are generally acceptable for unexposed applications. However, under Class 1 surface criteria:
1. Longitudinal scratches exceeding 0.05 mm (0.002 in.) in depth are rejectable for Class 1 automotive and exposed paneling.
2. Pitting exceeding 10% of nominal thickness mandates structural de-rating.
3. Severe rolled-in scale exceeding 5% surface area coverage impairs coating adhesion and requires surface dressing or coil rejection.

## Section 3: Verification and Non-Destructive Testing
Automated optical surface inspection provides preliminary anomaly detection. Confirmatory testing requires:
- Profilometry measurement of depth (Ra/Rz) across scratch boundaries.
- Eddy Current Testing (ET) to evaluate whether crazing micro-cracks penetrate into subsurface core grain boundaries.
- Visual inspection under raking angled illumination (15-30 degrees) to verify defect topography.
